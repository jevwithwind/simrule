SOURCE 09: LIMITATIONS AND WHEN TO CONSULT A HUMAN

Disclaimer: academic prototype for Ivey Online coursework; not affiliated with or representative of FSRA; fictional data; not legal advice.

WHAT THE SIMRULE REGULATORY ASSISTANT CAN DO
- Explain Take All Comers, decline rules and the difference from eligibility rules.
- Explain the four Levels and 15 criteria, and how the scoring code turns findings into a recommendation.
- Explain the six principles and fair consumer outcomes.
- Explain common rejection reasons and the issue codes, with what an insurer could do about each.
- Describe what a filing should include, by rule type.
- Walk through how a rule would be evaluated against Levels 1 to 4, step by step.
- Explain the review workflow, where humans validate, override or approve, and when senior sign-off is needed.
- Point to the official legislation and guidance pages.

WHAT IT CANNOT DO
- It cannot predict or decide whether a specific rule will be approved. Only the regulator's reviewers decide, after a full review that includes information the assistant does not have.
- It cannot give legal advice or interpret the law for a specific situation.
- It cannot cite section numbers. The legal references in its sources are held at instrument level because they could not be verified against the official text.
- It cannot see an insurer's actual filing, data or history, or the regulator's internal records.
- It does not know about changes to legislation or guidance after its sources were written.
- It cannot speak for the regulator. Simrule is an academic prototype and is not affiliated with or representative of FSRA.

WHEN TO CONSULT A HUMAN
Please contact the regulator directly, or a qualified professional, when:
- you want to know whether your specific rule will be approved;
- you need a legal interpretation of legislation, regulation or guidance;
- you disagree with a decision or want to dispute one;
- your situation involves human rights, privacy or another sensitive matter;
- the rule is unusual and not covered by the framework's examples;
- you need current requirements, deadlines or forms.

LIMITATIONS OF THE SIMRULE PROTOTYPE ITSELF
- The 100 AI assessments were generated once and frozen. New uploads get keyword triage (low confidence) and a similarity search only, unless a visitor runs the optional live assessment with their own API key.
- The scoring weights and thresholds were checked against 13 anchor rules, not against real regulatory decisions.
- Similarity search uses word overlap and structured features, not semantic understanding, so it can miss rules that mean the same thing in different words.
- There are no user accounts. Roles are a demonstration switch, not access control. Data is stored only in the visitor's browser.
- The prototype is in English only. A production service would also need French.
- Every insurer and precedent record is fictional. Precedent insurer names and approval years are illustrative.

RISKS THAT REQUIRE HUMAN OVERSIGHT
- Automation bias: reviewers may accept AI findings without reading them. Simrule requires a decision on every finding and shows how often findings are accepted without change.
- Missed proxy discrimination: a rule can look neutral and still affect a protected group. Reviewers should ask who is most affected.
- Stale or wrong citations: legal references can be outdated. Simrule marks unverified references.
- Gaming: insurers could word rules to avoid keyword flags. Reviewers judge substance, not wording.
- Drift: models, regulation and market conditions change. The consistency monitor and override patterns help detect drift.

HOW THE ASSISTANT SHOULD ANSWER WHEN UNSURE
If the sources do not cover a question, the assistant says so plainly and suggests consulting the regulator or a qualified professional. It does not guess.
