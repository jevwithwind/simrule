LEGISLATION
1. Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. I.8
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90i08
   Why it matters: the legal basis of Take All Comers. An insurer may decline auto insurance only on grounds filed with and approved by the regulator.

2. Regulation 664 under the Insurance Act (R.R.O. 1990, Reg. 664: Automobile Insurance)
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900664
   Why it matters: restricts certain factors in auto underwriting and risk classification. Simrule treats credit information and most lapses in coverage as restricted factors; confirm the current wording.

3. Human Rights Code, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.19
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19
   Why it matters: the right to equal treatment in services and contracts without discrimination on protected grounds, including race, ancestry, place of origin, colour, ethnic origin, citizenship, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, age, marital status, family status and disability. The Code has a provision on insurance contracts that allows automobile insurance to differentiate on age, sex, marital status, family status or disability only on reasonable and bona fide grounds. Other grounds, such as place of origin, citizenship, ancestry, ethnic origin, race and creed, are not covered by that provision. Indirect (constructive) discrimination is also covered by the Code. Confirm the exact wording in the official text.

4. Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms
   https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-12.html
   Why it matters: equality rights bind government action. They apply most directly to the regulator's own approval decision, not to a private insurer's conduct. Simrule mentions the Charter only where approving a rule would mean the regulator endorsing differential treatment on an equality-type ground.

5. Compulsory Automobile Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.25
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c25
   Why it matters: auto insurance is compulsory to drive in Ontario, so a decline directly affects a consumer's ability to drive legally.

6. Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario Act, 2016, S.O. 2016, c. 37, Sched. 8
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/16f37
   Why it matters: the regulator's objects include protecting the rights and interests of consumers. This grounds the Level 4 public policy filter.

7. Highway Traffic Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.8
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h08
   Why it matters: driver licensing (including graduated licences and licence classes), stunt driving and racing offences, demerit points and licence suspensions.

8. Motor Vehicle Accident Claims Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. M.41
   https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90m41
   Why it matters: a public fund of last resort for people injured by uninsured drivers, which shows the public cost when drivers cannot get insurance.

REGULATOR RULES AND GUIDANCE (FSRA)
9. FSRA Rule: Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices (UDAP)
   https://www.fsrao.ca/regulation/rules/insurance-rules/unfair-or-deceptive-acts-or-practices-rule
   Why it matters: declining auto insurance on grounds that are not filed, or using restricted information such as credit information, can be an unfair or deceptive act or practice.

10. Take-All-Comers information for industry
    https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/take-all-comers-information-industry
    Insurers must quote and accept every consumer who meets their approved underwriting rules.

11. Take-All-Comers thematic review report
    https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/publications/take-all-comers-thematic-review-report
    The regulator's review of insurer practices that make it harder for some consumers to get auto insurance quotes.

12. Filing Guidelines for Underwriting Rules
    https://www.fsrao.ca/filing-guidelines-underwriting-rules
    What an underwriting rule filing must contain. Simrule's prototype completeness checklist is based on it.

13. Underwriting technical notes
    https://www.fsrao.ca/underwriting-technical-notes
    Technical expectations for underwriting rule filings, including how rules and terms should be expressed.

14. Automobile Insurance Underwriting Rules and Risk Classification Variables
    https://www.fsrao.ca/automobile-insurance-underwriting-rules-and-risk-classification-variables
    Guidance on which variables may be used in underwriting rules and risk classification.

15. An Administrative Lapse or a Suspension of a Driver's Licence
    https://www.fsrao.ca/administrative-lapse-or-suspension-drivers-licence
    Administrative licence lapses or suspensions that are not caused by driving convictions (for example unpaid fines or medical reasons) are restricted in underwriting. Confirm the current conditions in the official text.

16. Regulation on lapse in automobile insurance coverage
    https://www.fsrao.ca/regulation-lapse-automobile-insurance-coverage
    A lapse in coverage may be considered only in limited circumstances. Confirm which circumstances in the official text.

17. Facility Association amendment to eligibility rules in the residual market
    https://www.fsrao.ca/facility-association-amendment-eligibility-rules-residual-market
    The Facility Association residual market is where drivers who cannot get voluntary-market coverage go, generally at a higher cost.

18. Fair Treatment of Consumers in Insurance
    https://www.fsrao.ca/fair-treatment-customers-insurance
    The expectation that insurers treat consumers fairly throughout the product life cycle, including clear disclosure.

19. Proposed Guidance: Automobile Insurance Rating and Underwriting Supervision Guidance
    https://www.fsrao.ca/industry/auto-insurance/regulatory-framework/guidance-auto-insurance/proposed-guidance-automobile-insurance-rating-and-underwriting-supervision-guidance
    Principles and fair consumer outcomes for rating and underwriting, including accurate pricing and freedom from unfair discrimination and proxies. This is the source of the six Level 2 principles.

20. Principles-based Regulation
    https://www.fsrao.ca/regulation/guidance/principles-based-regulation
    The regulator's outcomes-focused approach, which is why Level 2 tests principles rather than only rules.

HOW TO USE THIS LIST
- Cite the instrument, not a section number, unless you have checked the official text yourself.
- Guidance can change. Always check the date and status of a guidance page (for example, "proposed" guidance).
- For a specific filing, legal interpretation or a dispute, consult the regulator or a qualified professional.
