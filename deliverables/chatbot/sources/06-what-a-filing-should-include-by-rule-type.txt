SOURCE 06: WHAT A FILING SHOULD INCLUDE, BY RULE TYPE

Disclaimer: academic prototype for Ivey Online coursework; not affiliated with or representative of FSRA; fictional data; not legal advice. Check the regulator's official filing guidelines for current requirements; Simrule's checklist is a prototype and the guideline items are marked unverified.

SIMRULE'S PROTOTYPE COMPLETENESS CHECKLIST (12 ITEMS)
For each rule:
1. Submitting insurer identified.
2. Date submitted.
3. Proposed rule wording.
4. Insurer's stated rationale.
5. Rule category.
6. Confirmation that it is a decline rule, not an eligibility rule for optional coverage.
7. Definitions and explanations of key terms (from the regulator's filing guidelines, unverified).
8. Actuarial data or analysis supporting the rule. A statement that data exists is not the data.
9. How declined consumers are told the reason (from the Clear Consumer Communications principle).
For the filing package:
10. Summary of information form (filing guidelines, unverified).
11. Certificate of an official (filing guidelines, unverified).
12. Consolidated list of the insurer's underwriting rules (filing guidelines, unverified).

WHAT EVERY RULE SHOULD STATE CLEARLY
- The trigger, in objective and verifiable terms.
- Any threshold (how many events) and lookback period (how far back), with the reason for each.
- Who the rule applies to: the named insured, every listed driver, or the vehicle.
- Definitions of every key term.
- The data that links the trigger to claims frequency or severity.
- How a declined consumer is told the reason and can correct an error.

DRIVING RECORD RULES (accidents, convictions, suspensions)
Include: the type of event (at-fault accident, minor or major conviction, Criminal Code conviction, suspension), the number, the lookback period, and whether it counts for every driver on the policy.
Approved examples in the framework: 2 or more at-fault accidents in 3 years; 1 or more Criminal Code convictions in 3 years; combinations of minor convictions and non-payment cancellations within 3 years.
Watch for: charges rather than convictions (ALLEGATION_NOT_CONVICTION); lookbacks longer or thresholds lower than comparable approved rules without data (ARBITRARY_THRESHOLD); suspensions caused by administrative reasons rather than driving, which guidance restricts (PROHIBITED_FACTOR).

VEHICLE USE RULES (commercial use, racing, delivery, rideshare)
Include: a clear definition of the use (for example "commercial purposes" or "racing"), whether occasional use counts, and why standard personal auto coverage does not fit.
Approved examples: any automobile used for commercial purposes; used on a racetrack or in any race, speed test or competition; vehicles used for police work; vehicles used for a purpose they were not designed for.
Watch for: undefined frequency words such as "occasional" or "regular" (VAGUE_LANGUAGE); declining when an endorsement or commercial policy referral would serve the consumer better (LESS_RESTRICTIVE_ALTERNATIVE).

VEHICLE TYPE RULES (modifications, rebuilt, salvage, value, age, theft risk)
Include: an objective list or definition of the modifications or vehicle status, and how the insurer verifies it.
Approved examples: any vehicle modified for speed or used for racing; any vehicle with unacceptable customizations or modifications (the filing should define "unacceptable"); declining rebuilt vehicles is common approved practice.
Watch for: theft-risk rules such as "top stolen models", which the framework gives as a non-compliant example; rating or anti-theft requirements are the usual alternative. Undefined terms such as "high-performance" or "exotic" (VAGUE_LANGUAGE).

CLAIMS AND FRAUD HISTORY RULES
Include: whether the trigger is a conviction or a final finding, the lookback period, and the definition of fraud or misrepresentation used.
Approved example: 1 or more convictions for auto insurance fraud in the preceding 10 years. A previous fraud conviction is common approved practice.
Watch for: suspicion, investigation or a special investigations unit referral without a final outcome (ALLEGATION_NOT_CONVICTION); counting not-at-fault claims, which do not reflect the applicant's driving (NOT_RISK_RELEVANT).

PAYMENT HISTORY RULES (non-payment cancellations, outstanding premium)
Include: the number of cancellations for non-payment, the lookback period, and whether the debt is owed to this insurer or its affiliates.
Approved examples: failure to pay outstanding premium owed to the insurer or its affiliates for the same named insured; combinations of non-payment cancellations with convictions or accidents.
Watch for: credit scores, bankruptcy, collections or other financial information, which guidance restricts (PROHIBITED_FACTOR); debts owed to unrelated companies.

LICENSING RULES
Include: the licence requirement, who must hold it, and how licences from other provinces or countries are treated.
Approved examples: no driver of the insured vehicle holds a valid Canadian licence; improper class of licence or invalid licence.
Watch for: years since first licensed, graduated licence stages or new drivers (DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_DRIVERS); declining foreign licences instead of verifying foreign driving history (DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_TO_CANADA); administrative lapses such as unpaid fines (PROHIBITED_FACTOR).

GEOGRAPHIC RULES (postal code, region, where the vehicle is kept)
Include: the exact geographic definition, the data linking it to claims, and why rating by territory is not enough.
There is no approved geographic decline rule in the framework's examples. Location is a recognised rating factor, so a full decline is hard to justify.
Watch for: postal codes or neighbourhoods that track place of origin, ethnic origin or income (PROXY_FOR_PROTECTED_GROUND); access to mandatory coverage for whole communities (ACCESSIBILITY_IMPACT); a rating approach as the less restrictive option (LESS_RESTRICTIVE_ALTERNATIVE).

STANDARD CONDITIONS SIMRULE ATTACHES TO "APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS"
1. Supporting actuarial analysis must be filed before the rule is used. No submission in Simrule's test set includes actuarial data.
2. Key terms must be defined in the filing.
3. The insurer must tell declined consumers the specific reason and how to correct inaccurate information.
