SOURCE 01: ABOUT SIMRULE AND WHAT IT COVERS

Disclaimer: Simrule is an academic prototype built for Ivey Online coursework. It is not affiliated with or representative of the Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA). All insurer submissions and precedent records in Simrule are fictional. Nothing in this assistant is legal advice.

WHAT SIMRULE IS
Simrule is a review workbench for auto insurance underwriting decline rules. It imagines how a regulatory analyst at Ontario's auto insurance regulator could use AI as a partner when insurers file proposed decline rules. The AI structures the filing, checks it against a fixed framework, flags concerns, compares it with similar rules, and drafts a rationale. A human reviewer validates every finding, makes the decision and owns the rationale.

The Simrule Regulatory Assistant (this chatbot) answers questions about the framework, the review process and what a filing should contain. It serves two audiences: reviewers inside the regulator, and insurer applicants preparing a filing. It explains how a rule would be evaluated. It never predicts or decides whether a specific rule will be approved.

THE FIVE PARTS OF THE SIMRULE APP
1. Document upload (Intake). An insurer filing can be uploaded as PDF, DOCX, TXT or Markdown, pasted, or typed into a form. Simrule extracts each rule and its labelled fields. The reviewer checks and confirms the extraction before anything else runs.
2. Compliance dashboard. A queue of 100 fictional submissions from 10 fictional insurers, with the AI recommendation, score, Level 1 hard stops, consumer impact and age in queue.
3. Flagging system. Each rule is assessed against 15 criteria grouped into four Levels. Each finding has a status, a severity, issue codes, short reasoning, verbatim evidence quotes and citations.
4. Recommendation panel. Shows the "AI recommendation (advisory)" with the score breakdown, evidence, similar rules, a consumer lens and next steps.
5. Decision workflow. The reviewer accepts or overrides each finding, chooses a decision, drafts a rationale, and confirms ownership of it. Some decisions need senior sign-off. Every action is written to an audit trail.

WHAT IS IN SCOPE
Simrule assesses underwriting decline rules only. A decline rule is the insurer's right to decline to offer an auto insurance policy to a consumer.

WHAT IS OUT OF SCOPE
Underwriting eligibility rules are out of scope. An eligibility rule sets criteria for optional coverages or coverage limits. If the consumer does not meet the criteria, the insurer may decline that optional coverage or limit, but not the whole policy. Examples: declining optional collision coverage on an older vehicle, or limiting third-party liability limits above a certain amount. Simrule labels these "Out of scope" with the issue code OUT_OF_SCOPE_ELIGIBILITY and routes them to the eligibility-rule review process.

THE CORE DESIGN IDEA: JUDGMENT IS SEPARATE FROM SCORING
In Simrule, the AI produces findings only: for each of 15 criteria it records a status (meets standard, concern, does not meet, insufficient information), a severity (low, medium, high), issue codes, reasoning, evidence and citations. The AI never writes the recommendation. Fixed, published scoring code turns the findings into one of four recommendations: Approve with conditions, Request more information, Recommend decline, or Out of scope. Same findings, same answer, every time. This makes every recommendation explainable and gives reviewers a precise place to disagree.

THE FOUR PRINCIPLES SIMRULE IS BUILT ON
1. Human-in-the-loop: AI augments, never replaces. Reviewers validate, override or approve every recommendation.
2. Transparency: show why something was flagged or recommended, not just that it was.
3. Consistency: similar applications are treated similarly.
4. Consumer protection: keep the consumer visible throughout.

THE DATA IN THE PROTOTYPE
- 100 fictional decline rules from 10 fictional insurers, submitted between June and November 2024. No submission includes actuarial data.
- 100 AI assessments, generated once with a versioned prompt (version 2), validated against a schema, and checked in a consistency audit.
- A precedent registry of 25 entries: 14 approved rules from the framework (shown with invented insurer names and illustrative years), 6 common approved practices, and 5 illustrative non-compliant examples.
- A citation registry. Every legal reference is held at instrument level and marked "section not verified" because the official sources could not be checked from the build environment.

WHAT THE RECOMMENDATIONS MEAN
- Approve with conditions: the rule appears to meet the framework. Approval should carry conditions, such as filing supporting actuarial analysis, defining key terms, and telling declined consumers the reason and how to correct errors.
- Request more information: the rule might be acceptable, but a gap could change the outcome. The reviewer asks the insurer specific questions.
- Recommend decline: the rule fails a Level 1 requirement, has a high-severity problem, or scores below the threshold.
- Out of scope: the rule is an eligibility rule and goes to a different process.

Every recommendation is advisory. Only a human decision creates an outcome.
