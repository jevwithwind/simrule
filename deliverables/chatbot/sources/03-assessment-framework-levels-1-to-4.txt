SOURCE 03: THE ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK, LEVELS 1 TO 4

Disclaimer: academic prototype for Ivey Online coursework; not affiliated with or representative of FSRA; fictional data; not legal advice. This explains how a rule would be evaluated; it does not predict whether any specific rule will be approved.

OVERVIEW
Every proposed decline rule is assessed at four Levels, with 15 criteria in total. The Levels come from the course framework "Auto Insurance Underwriting Rule Assessment: A Regulatory Evaluation Framework". Level 1 is required basic compliance. Levels 2 to 4 are the stretch scope.

For each criterion the AI records:
- a status: Meets standard, Concern, Does not meet standard, or Insufficient information;
- a severity: low, medium or high;
- one or more issue codes for any problem;
- 2 to 4 sentences of reasoning;
- evidence: exact words quoted from the rule or the insurer's rationale;
- citations from the citation registry.

LEVEL 1: BASIC COMPLIANCE (REQUIRED, A GATE)
What is tested: the Acts, legislation, regulation and guidance; the Human Rights Code and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms; and whether the rule already exists in the market.
Result expected: if not compliant, refer to the relevant Act, regulation or guidance. If the rule already exists, reference that rule by another insurer and the year it was approved.
Role in scoring: a gate. Any Level 1 criterion that does not meet the standard is a full-stop recommendation to decline (a "Level 1 hard stop"). Level 1 does not add to the score.

Criteria:
- L1_LEGISLATION, Legislation, regulation and guidance: Is the rule compliant with the Insurance Act, its regulations, the Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices (UDAP) Rule and published FSRA guidance?
- L1_HUMAN_RIGHTS, Human Rights Code and Charter: Does the rule use a protected ground, or a close proxy for one, outside what the Human Rights Code allows for insurance contracts?
- L1_MARKET, Already exists in the market: Has the same or a very similar rule been approved for another insurer? If so, which insurer and when?

How the market check is read:
- Meets standard: an approved rule with the same or similar intent exists in the precedent registry.
- Insufficient information (low): no same or similar approved rule was found. The rule is assessed on its own merits.
- Concern: the closest match is an illustrative non-compliant example, or the matching precedent may no longer meet current standards.

LEVEL 2: INTERPRETATION OF PRINCIPLES AND FAIR CONSUMER OUTCOMES
What is tested: the six principles and fair consumer outcomes in the framework appendix (see source 04).
Result expected: list any problematic aspects and explain what does not seem appropriate.
Role in scoring: weight 40 out of 100.

Criteria:
- L2_ACCURATE, Accurate pricing and underwriting: Does the rule reflect the individual applicant's risk profile?
- L2_NO_DISCRIMINATION, Absence of unfair discrimination, bias or proxies: Is the rule free of direct or indirect use of protected grounds or proxies for them?
- L2_ACCESSIBLE, Accessible products and coverages: Would the rule unfairly limit consumers' access to mandatory auto insurance?
- L2_COST_MITIGATION, Cost mitigation: Does the rule avoid passing unnecessary or excessive costs to consumers, including declined consumers?
- L2_BALANCED, Balanced profitability and consumer interests: Does the rule balance the insurer's profitability with fair treatment of consumers?
- L2_CLEAR_COMMUNICATION, Clear consumer communications: Could a consumer understand why they were declined and what they can do about it?

LEVEL 3: CHECK THAT THE RISK IS NOT SUBJECTIVE OR ARBITRARY AND HAS A DIRECT RELATIONSHIP WITH THE RISK
What is tested: Does the insurer have actuarial data or analysis? How did it define the rule and what informed it? Can it show a clear link to driver behaviour or claims exposure?
Role in scoring: weight 35 out of 100.

Criteria:
- L3_NOT_SUBJECTIVE, Not subjective: Is the rule applied on objective, verifiable facts rather than insurer judgment?
- L3_NOT_ARBITRARY, Not arbitrary: Are the thresholds and lookback periods reasoned rather than arbitrary?
- L3_RISK_LINK, Direct relationship to risk: Is there a clear, direct link to driver behaviour or claims exposure?
- L3_ACTUARIAL, Actuarial support: Has the insurer supplied actuarial data or analysis that supports the rule?
- L3_DEFINITION, Definition clarity: Are the terms defined clearly enough to be applied the same way every time?

A note on actuarial support: missing actuarial analysis on its own is not a reason to decline. It is recorded as Insufficient information with the issue code NO_ACTUARIAL_SUPPORT. The severity is low when a same or similar approved precedent exists and the rule is no stricter, or when the rule rests on a legal or administrative requirement. It is medium when the rule is new and rests on a statistical claim, or is stricter than the precedent. It is never high.

LEVEL 4: PUBLIC POLICY FILTER
What is tested: as a government agency responsible for protecting consumers and ensuring fair treatment, is there anything about this proposal that does not seem appropriate?
Result expected: yes, no or maybe, with an explanation.
Role in scoring: weight 25 out of 100.

Criterion:
- L4_PUBLIC_POLICY, Public policy filter. The status maps to the framework's answer:
  - Meets standard = "No: nothing about the proposal appears inappropriate."
  - Concern = "Maybe: some aspects need justification."
  - Insufficient information = "Maybe: cannot judge without more information."
  - Does not meet standard = "Yes: the proposal conflicts with public policy priorities."

HOW THE SCORE AND RECOMMENDATION ARE CALCULATED
Simrule's scoring code, not the AI, calculates the recommendation.
Criterion values: Meets standard 1, Concern 0.5, Insufficient information 0.5, Does not meet standard 0.
Score = sum over Levels 2 to 4 of (Level weight x average criterion value at that Level). Weights: Level 2 40, Level 3 35, Level 4 25. Maximum 100.

The rules are applied in this order:
1. The rule is an eligibility rule: Out of scope.
2. Any Level 1 criterion does not meet the standard: Recommend decline (hard stop).
3. Any high-severity "does not meet standard" at Levels 2 to 4: Recommend decline.
4. Score below 50: Recommend decline.
5. Score 50 to 74, or any medium or high severity Insufficient information finding at Levels 2 to 4: Request more information.
6. Score 75 or above: Approve with conditions.

Senior review is flagged when AI confidence is low or any Level 1 criterion is a Concern.

WALKING THROUGH A RULE (HOW TO THINK ABOUT IT)
1. Is it a decline rule or an eligibility rule?
2. Level 1: does it conflict with legislation or guidance, use a protected ground or a close proxy, or match an approved or non-compliant precedent?
3. Level 2: is it accurate to the individual's risk, free of proxies, does it keep access to mandatory coverage, avoid unnecessary cost, balance interests, and could a consumer understand it?
4. Level 3: is it objective, are the threshold and lookback reasoned, is the link to driving risk direct, is there data, are terms defined?
5. Level 4: is there anything a consumer protection regulator should not approve?
The answer to each step is a finding a human reviewer must accept or override. The outcome is decided by a person.
