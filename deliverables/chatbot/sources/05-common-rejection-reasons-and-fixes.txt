SOURCE 05: COMMON REJECTION REASONS, ISSUE CODES AND HOW TO FIX THEM

THE SIX REASONS THE REGULATOR MAY REJECT A PROPOSED RULE (FROM THE FRAMEWORK)
1. Not risk-relevant: the rule is not based on any characteristic of the insured that is relevant to the risk. Issue codes: NOT_RISK_RELEVANT, WEAK_RISK_LINK.
2. Vague language: the wording is vague or ambiguous and invites many interpretations. Issue codes: VAGUE_LANGUAGE, OVERBROAD_SCOPE, SUBJECTIVE_JUDGMENT.
3. Copycat rule: the rule inappropriately refers to the actions of another insurer. Issue code: COPYCAT_REFERENCE.
4. Human rights violation: the rule uses grounds that breach the Human Rights Code or the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. Issue codes: HUMAN_RIGHTS_PROTECTED_GROUND, PROXY_FOR_PROTECTED_GROUND.
5. Contrary to legislation: the rule conflicts with current legislation such as the Insurance Act, the UDAP Rule or published FSRA guidance. Issue codes: CONTRARY_TO_LEGISLATION, PROHIBITED_FACTOR.
6. Discriminatory impact: the rule may decline newly licensed drivers or drivers new to Canada. Issue codes: DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_DRIVERS, DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_TO_CANADA.

THE 25 ISSUE CODES SIMRULE USES, WITH WHAT AN INSURER COULD DO
Level 1 codes
- OUT_OF_SCOPE_ELIGIBILITY (eligibility rule, not a decline rule): refile through the eligibility-rule process for optional coverages or limits.
- CONTRARY_TO_LEGISLATION (contrary to legislation or published guidance): withdraw the rule or redraft it so it is consistent with the Insurance Act, the UDAP Rule and FSRA guidance.
- PROHIBITED_FACTOR (uses a factor that regulation or guidance restricts): remove the restricted factor, for example credit information, a lapse in coverage or an administrative licence lapse, or limit it to the uses the rules allow.
- HUMAN_RIGHTS_PROTECTED_GROUND (uses a protected ground under the Human Rights Code): remove the protected ground. Where the Code's insurance provision may apply (age, sex, marital status, family status, disability), show reasonable and bona fide grounds with data.
- PRECEDENT_APPROVED_MATCH (matches an approved precedent): no change needed for the match itself. Supply supporting analysis, because approval for another insurer is a consideration, not a guarantee.
- PRECEDENT_NON_COMPLIANT_MATCH (resembles an illustrative non-compliant example): explain what makes the rule materially different, or withdraw it.
- PENDING_SIMILAR_SUBMISSION (a similar submission is pending from another insurer): no insurer action. Reviewers assess the similar submissions together for consistency.

Level 2 codes
- PROXY_FOR_PROTECTED_GROUND (acts as a proxy for a protected ground): replace the proxy with a direct, behaviour-based risk factor and test the rule for disparate impact.
- DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_DRIVERS (declines newly licensed or inexperienced drivers): address inexperience through rating, not a decline, so new drivers can still buy mandatory coverage.
- DISCRIMINATORY_IMPACT_NEW_TO_CANADA (declines drivers who are new to Canada): accept verifiable foreign driving history and rate for limited Canadian experience instead of declining.
- LESS_RESTRICTIVE_ALTERNATIVE (a less restrictive tool would address the risk): use rating, an endorsement, a driver exclusion or a verification step instead of a full decline.
- ACCESSIBILITY_IMPACT (limits access to mandatory auto insurance): show that affected consumers keep reasonable access to coverage, or narrow the rule.
- UNFAIR_COST_SHIFT (shifts unnecessary cost to consumers): show the rule does not push consumers into higher-cost markets without a matching risk reason.
- UNCLEAR_CONSUMER_COMMUNICATION (a consumer could not easily understand or contest the decline): explain how declined consumers will be told the reason, the evidence used and how to correct errors.

Level 3 codes
- ALLEGATION_NOT_CONVICTION (relies on an allegation, charge or investigation): base the rule on convictions or other final, verifiable outcomes.
- NOT_RISK_RELEVANT (not based on a risk-relevant characteristic): tie the rule to driving behaviour, vehicle use or claims exposure, or withdraw it.
- WEAK_RISK_LINK (link to driving risk is indirect or weak): provide data showing a direct link to claims frequency or severity, or narrow the rule to the risky behaviour itself.
- NO_ACTUARIAL_SUPPORT (no actuarial data or analysis supplied): submit the analysis that supports the trigger, threshold and lookback period.
- VAGUE_LANGUAGE (vague or undefined terms): define each key term so the rule is applied the same way to every applicant.
- SUBJECTIVE_JUDGMENT (depends on subjective insurer judgment): replace judgment calls with objective, verifiable criteria.
- OVERBROAD_SCOPE (reaches more consumers than the stated risk justifies): narrow the rule to the specific behaviour or condition that drives the risk.
- ARBITRARY_THRESHOLD (threshold or lookback appears arbitrary): explain how the threshold and lookback were chosen and show the supporting data.
- COPYCAT_REFERENCE (relies on another insurer's actions or on "industry practice"): justify the rule on your own risk analysis, not on another insurer's decision or general practice.

Level 4 codes
- PRIVACY_INTRUSION (requires intrusive collection of personal information): remove the need to collect sensitive information that is not necessary to assess driving risk.
- PUBLIC_POLICY_CONFLICT (conflicts with public policy priorities): withdraw the rule. It penalises conduct or circumstances that public policy protects or encourages.

THE MOST FREQUENT ISSUES IN SIMRULE'S 100 FICTIONAL SUBMISSIONS
Across the 100 fictional rules, the codes found on the most rules were: NO_ACTUARIAL_SUPPORT (all 100, because no submission included data), ACCESSIBILITY_IMPACT (88 rules), VAGUE_LANGUAGE (66), PUBLIC_POLICY_CONFLICT (63) and UNFAIR_COST_SHIFT (59). These counts describe the fictional test set only.

PATTERNS THAT OFTEN CAUSE PROBLEMS
- Charges, accusations or investigations instead of convictions.
- Language, place of birth, citizenship, newcomer status or neighbourhood used as a trigger.
- Receipt of social assistance, disability benefits or other income support.
- Credit scores, bankruptcy or other financial information.
- Lapses in coverage, never having had insurance, or switching insurers often.
- Words such as "excessive", "high-risk", "unacceptable" or "frequent" without a definition.
- Rationales that say "other insurers do this" or "industry practice".
- Rules that rely on a personal judgment by an employee or broker.

PATTERNS THAT TEND TO BE CLEARER
- Convictions with a defined number and lookback period.
- At-fault accidents with a defined number and lookback period.
- Vehicle use that standard personal auto coverage was not designed for, such as racing or commercial use.
- Fraud convictions.
- Licensing requirements that apply to every driver of the vehicle.
Even these still need data and clear definitions, and each filing is reviewed on its own merits.
